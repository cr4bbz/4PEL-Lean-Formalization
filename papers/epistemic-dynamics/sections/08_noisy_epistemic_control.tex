\section{Noisy Identification and Epistemic Control}

Gate 116 removes the deterministic-identification idealization. The observation channel is now noisy: the same signal can occur in either hidden world, although with different likelihood. This breaks the equivalence between successful target decoding and logical elimination of alternatives.

\subsection{A symmetric noisy sensor}

The finite witness has two hidden worlds, positive and negative, and two possible signals. Under the positive world the positive signal has likelihood $4/5$ and the negative signal $1/5$; under the negative world these likelihoods are reversed. The prior over worlds is symmetric:
\[
P(W_+)=P(W_-)=\frac12.
\]
For an observed trace $x$, Gate 116 computes the exact Bayesian posterior
\[
P(W_+\mid x)
=
\frac{P(W_+)P(x\mid W_+)}
{P(W_+)P(x\mid W_+)+P(W_-)P(x\mid W_-)}.
\]
The likelihood model assumes known sensor reliability and conditional independence between repeated signals.

\subsection{One observation remains unresolved}

After one positive observation,
\[
P(W_+\mid +)=\frac45,
\]
and after one negative observation,
\[
P(W_+\mid -)=\frac15.
\]
Gate 116 introduces an explicit control threshold $9/10$ for converting posterior confidence into a qualitative action status. Values at or above $9/10$ map to $T$, values at or below $1/10$ map to $F$, and the intermediate region maps to $N$.

Consequently, either single observation remains unresolved:
\[
+\mapsto N,
\qquad
-\mapsto N.
\]
The sequential controller therefore requests another measurement after either possible first signal.

The $9/10$ threshold is a modelling convention. It is not a logical definition of truth and should not be read as one.

\subsection{Agreement crosses the control threshold}

Two agreeing positive signals produce
\[
P(W_+\mid ++)=\frac{16}{17},
\]
while two agreeing negative signals produce
\[
P(W_+\mid --)=\frac1{17}.
\]
The first exceeds $9/10$ and maps to $T$; the second lies below $1/10$ and maps to $F$. The controller therefore commits directionally after two agreeing observations.

Conflicting observations cancel in the symmetric witness:
\[
P(W_+\mid +-)=P(W_+\mid -+)=\frac12,
\]
so both traces map back to $N$ and the controller preserves unresolvedness.

\subsection{Control confidence is not logical certainty}

The decisive distinction appears after the apparently strong trace $++$. Although the posterior exceeds the control threshold, the negative world still assigns that trace positive likelihood:
\[
P(++\mid W_-)=\frac1{25}>0.
\]
Thus the controller may rationally cross its encoded action threshold without eliminating the alternative world. Gate 116 also proves that no deterministic decoder from a single signal can be truth-guaranteeing on channel support, because every individual signal remains possible in both target-different worlds.

The resulting separation is
\[
\boxed{
\text{confidence sufficient for control}
\neq
\text{logical elimination of alternatives}
}.
\]
This is not a defect in Bayesian updating or in four-valued representation. It is the expected consequence of fallible evidence. The posterior quantifies relative support under the sensor model; the qualitative $T/F/N$ status is a chosen control abstraction over that posterior; logical impossibility is a stronger condition again.

\subsection{From four-valued status to a layered epistemic architecture}

Gate 116 therefore makes explicit a distinction that earlier gates only approached indirectly. The formal system now contains at least four separable objects:
\[
\text{hidden world},
\qquad
\text{quantitative belief},
\qquad
\text{four-valued control status},
\qquad
\text{action}.
\]
None is identified with the others. A world may be positive while the agent remains at $N$ after one noisy observation. The posterior may later exceed a control threshold and yield $T$ even though the negative world remains logically possible. The action policy may then commit because the encoded decision rule treats that residual risk as acceptable.

This layered architecture provides the clearest current interpretation of 4PEL in the project: the four-valued component functions as a qualitative epistemic control layer embedded between richer evidence and action, rather than as a replacement for quantitative belief or as an infallible detector of metaphysical truth.

\paragraph{Boundary.}
Gate 116 does not yet price repeated measurement, infer sensor reliability, represent correlated noise, or optimize stopping time. The next natural formal question is therefore whether \texttt{measureAgain} remains optimal once each additional noisy observation has a cost and only probabilistic expected benefit.
