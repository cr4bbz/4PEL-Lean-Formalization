# Philosophical Case Studies in 4-PEL

This document turns the existing paradox modules into a research programme rather than a catalogue of examples.

## Method

Each case study should separate four layers:

1. **Object-level semantics** — which propositions receive positive and negative support?
2. **Epistemic policy** — which support levels cross the Lockean belief threshold?
3. **Aggregation principles** — which closure, consistency, or introspection rules are additionally imposed?
4. **Philosophical diagnosis** — which layer actually generates the paradoxical result?

The target is not merely to show that 4-PEL can represent a paradox. The target is to identify assumptions that are necessary, sufficient, dispensable, or jointly responsible for the paradox.

The workflow mirrors the structural method used in `goedel-4pel`:

```text
informal paradox
      |
      v
signed / probabilistic reconstruction
      |
      v
assumption decomposition
      |
      v
finite models and countermodels
      |
      v
minimality / recovery questions
      |
      v
philosophical interpretation
```

## Case Study 1 — Preface Paradox

### Informal problem

A careful author believes every individual assertion in a book, while also believing, on the basis of ordinary fallibility, that at least one assertion in the book is false.

Let the representative claims be `p1`, `p2`, and `p3`.

The classical-looking pressure is:

```text
B(p1)
B(p2)
B(p3)
B(not (p1 and p2 and p3))
```

Together with conjunctive closure of belief:

```text
B(p1) and B(p2) and B(p3)
    -> B(p1 and p2 and p3)
```

this generates the familiar tension.

### 4-PEL control model

`PEL4/Paradoxes/Preface.lean` uses four equiprobable worlds:

```text
allCorrect : p1=T, p2=T, p3=T
error1     : p1=F, p2=T, p3=T
error2     : p1=T, p2=F, p3=T
error3     : p1=T, p2=T, p3=F
```

with Lockean threshold

```text
c = 3/4.
```

Hence every individual claim has positive probability `3/4`, but the conjunction has positive probability only `1/4` and negative probability `3/4`.

The intended evaluations are therefore:

```text
B(p1) = T
B(p2) = T
B(p3) = T

B(p1 and p2 and p3) = F
B(not (p1 and p2 and p3)) = T
```

The first philosophical result is therefore diagnostic rather than revisionary:

> Threshold belief alone does not generate an object-level contradiction. The Preface pressure appears when local threshold acceptance is combined with a conjunctive closure principle that treats individually accepted claims as jointly accepted.

### Research hypotheses

#### H1 — Threshold-source hypothesis

The paradox is generated by Lockean threshold belief itself.

**Current status:** disfavoured. The threshold model produces local acceptance plus global fallibility without itself deriving belief in the conjunction.

#### H2 — Closure-source hypothesis

The paradox is generated by conjunctive closure of belief rather than by the threshold rule alone.

**Current status:** supported by the control model and strengthened by P1 and P2. The Preface region persists across a whole interval of Lockean thresholds and survives arbitrary asymmetry in the one-error distribution.

#### H3 — Rational-glut hypothesis

The correct representation of the Preface state is a four-valued epistemic glut.

**Status:** open. The current models deliberately keep the object-level valuation classical and therefore show that a glut is not required merely to represent the Preface configuration. A later model should test whether a glut becomes appropriate at a meta-level that records incompatible acceptance policies.

#### H4 — Two-kinds-of-incoherence hypothesis

4-PEL can distinguish:

```text
object-level inconsistency
```

from

```text
policy-induced / inferential incoherence.
```

**Status:** strengthened. P1 and P2 show that policy-induced incoherence occupies a stable geometric region even when every object-level valuation remains classical.

## P1 — Symmetric threshold boundary

`PEL4/Paradoxes/PrefaceBoundary.lean` generalizes the control case to `n` claims.

The symmetric family contains `n + 1` equiprobable worlds:

```text
1 all-correct world
n one-error worlds
```

For every individual claim `p_i`:

```text
P(p_i) = n / (n + 1)
```

while:

```text
P(p_1 and ... and p_n) = 1 / (n + 1)
P(not (p_1 and ... and p_n)) = n / (n + 1).
```

Therefore the characteristic Preface configuration occurs whenever the Lockean threshold satisfies:

```text
1/2 < c <= n/(n+1)
```

for `n >= 2`.

Equivalently, for a threshold represented as `k/(n+1)`, the denominator-free region is:

```text
2*k > n+1
k <= n
```

Lean proves `symmetric_preface_boundary`, deriving local acceptance, rejection of the total conjunction, and acceptance of fallibility throughout this region. Lean also proves `symmetric_preface_region_nonempty_iff`, showing that the symmetric Preface region is non-empty exactly when `n >= 2`.

### P1 philosophical result

This blocks one tempting diagnosis of the Preface paradox:

> The tension is not an artefact of a pathological threshold value.

For every finite `n >= 2`, there is a non-empty strict-majority interval of Lockean thresholds where all individual claims are accepted, the conjunction is rejected, and fallibility is accepted.

The upper boundary is:

```text
c_max(n) = n/(n+1) = 1 - 1/(n+1).
```

Hence `c_max(n)` tends to `1` as `n` grows. Increasing the size of the corpus does not squeeze the Preface phenomenon toward permissive belief thresholds. Sufficiently large corpora permit the Preface configuration even under thresholds arbitrarily close to certainty.

The structural culprit is the gap between **marginal acceptance** and **joint acceptance**, not low epistemic standards.

## P2 — Asymmetric error distributions and simplex geometry

`PEL4/Paradoxes/PrefaceSimplex.lean` removes the symmetry assumption for the three-claim one-error family.

Write:

```text
a  = P(all claims correct)
e1 = P(only p1 is false)
e2 = P(only p2 is false)
e3 = P(only p3 is false)
```

with:

```text
a + e1 + e2 + e3 = 1.
```

At Lockean threshold `c`, local acceptance of each claim is equivalent to:

```text
e1 <= 1-c
e2 <= 1-c
e3 <= 1-c
```

and acceptance of global fallibility is equivalent to:

```text
a <= 1-c.
```

Thus the entire three-claim Preface region is the intersection:

```text
Delta^3 ∩ {a,e1,e2,e3 <= 1-c}
```

where `Delta^3` is the probability simplex.

This gives an immediate geometric necessity condition. Four coordinates, each at most `1-c`, must still sum to `1`:

```text
1 <= 4(1-c)
```

so:

```text
c <= 3/4.
```

The Lean theorem `preface_simplex3_threshold_necessary` proves the denominator-free version:

```text
4*k <= 3*m
```

for arbitrary finite masses `a,e1,e2,e3` summing to `m`. The companion theorem `preface_simplex3_above_three_quarters_empty` proves that any threshold strictly above `3/4` makes this whole asymmetric one-error region empty.

### P2 philosophical result

The symmetric model did not manufacture the threshold boundary. It merely sits on it.

The `3/4` ceiling is a **geometric packing constraint**: rational local acceptance and rational global fallibility require four mutually exclusive epistemic possibilities to fit beneath the same coordinate cap `1-c` while their masses still sum to one.

This suggests a stronger diagnosis of the Preface paradox:

> The paradox is governed by the geometry of how fallibility mass can be distributed across mutually exclusive error possibilities, not by symmetry of the examples.

For `n` claims, the analogous one-error simplex has `n+1` coordinates. The same packing argument predicts the general boundary:

```text
c <= n/(n+1).
```

P1 supplies a symmetric witness at that boundary; P2 shows, for three claims, that arbitrary asymmetry cannot exceed it. The next formal target is the general `n`-dimensional necessity theorem.

## Next Preface experiments

### P2b — General simplex theorem

Generalize the geometric packing argument from four coordinates to `n+1` coordinates and prove:

```text
one-error Preface region nonempty
-> c <= n/(n+1).
```

Together with P1, this would give an exact necessary-and-sufficient threshold characterization for the entire one-error family.

### P3 — Signed evidence

Introduce genuine `B` and `N` values at the object level rather than embedding a classical valuation into FDE.

Question:

```text
Does four-valued evidence enlarge the Preface region,
shrink it, or divide it into philosophically distinct subregions?
```

### P4 — Closure variants

Compare at least:

```text
full conjunctive closure
pairwise closure
bounded-k closure
closure only for independent claims
closure only above a stronger threshold
```

The research target is the weakest closure principle that recreates the contradiction pressure.

### P5 — Recovery theorem

Seek a theorem of the form:

```text
LocalAcceptance
+ ClosureCondition X
+ GlobalFallibility
-> epistemic conflict
```

and then prove countermodels when components of `X` are removed.

This is the direct analogue of the assumption-minimality method in `goedel-4pel`.

## Planned sequence

1. Preface — aggregation and fallibility.
2. Lottery — threshold belief and conjunction.
3. Fitch — knowability and modal collapse.
4. Moore — self-referential belief/content interaction.
5. Contrary-to-Duty — normative conflict and gaps/gluts.

The central criterion for success is always the same:

> A case study earns philosophical value only when the formal model reveals a distinction, dependency, boundary, or countermodel that is not already visible from the informal statement of the paradox.
