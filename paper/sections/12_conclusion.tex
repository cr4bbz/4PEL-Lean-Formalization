\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The dynamic 4-PEL development begins with a simple threshold semantics and ends with a surprisingly structured phase picture. Positive and negative evidence are tracked independently, so probabilistic belief is a pair of Lockean threshold decisions. Evidence-stable knowledge is exactly that belief value filtered by qualitative homogeneity of the complete accessible FDE profile.

Admissible conditionalization changes only probability, yet its categorical effects are maximally expressive. A six-world family realizes every ordered transition between \(\Tval,\Fval,\Bval,\Nval\) at the level of \(\Know(\Bel p)\). Gluts and gaps can both be created and removed. This global reachability does not make the dynamics arbitrary. Pointwise belief invariance is characterized exactly by preservation of the two threshold bits.

Those bits supply a natural Boolean geometry. The four FDE values form a threshold square, and the number of changed coordinates gives a wall count of zero, one, or two. Numerical endpoint analysis then identifies a changed bit with straddling of the corresponding support threshold. The affine interpolation of rational support masses turns straddling into a literal threshold-hit theorem with an explicit parameter in the unit interval.

For diagonal two-wall transitions, the two support coordinates have unique crossing times. Their order adds temporal structure absent from the endpoints. Simultaneous crossing is exactly passage through \((c,c)\). Sequential crossing forces an adjacent intermediate phase at the rational midpoint. Consequently, a diagonal endpoint transition has a short hidden phase history. For \(\Nval\to\Bval\), positive-first motion yields \(\Nval\to\Tval\to\Bval\), while negative-first motion yields \(\Nval\to\Fval\to\Bval\). The remaining diagonals obey the corresponding symmetric table.

The main verified dependency chain is therefore
\[
\boxed{
\text{probabilistic support}
\to
\text{threshold side}
\to
\text{categorical wall}
\to
\text{affine crossing time}
\to
\text{crossing order}
\to
\text{intermediate FDE phase}.}
\]

This chain turns the four-valued endpoint space into more than a classification table. It gives the beginning of a kinematics of epistemic phases.

The next step is also clear. The present path lives in support space. To obtain a full model-dynamic theorem, one must characterize when that support trajectory can be lifted to a trajectory of admissible probabilistic models. Only after that lifting problem is understood should the development move to arbitrary continuous paths or measure-theoretic probability spaces.

The manuscript therefore closes at a useful boundary. The finite rational theory is already strong enough to expose complete reachability, exact robustness, wall geometry, event order, and forced intermediate phases. The stronger continuous and model-valued interpretations remain explicit research questions rather than assumptions hidden inside the formal results.
