\section{Phase Contacts under Persistent Evidence}
\label{app:persistent-phase-nerve}

The universal construction of Appendix~\ref{app:phase-nerve} allows arbitrary
value profiles. A further condition changes the result sharply. Define the
information order by
\[
 a\leq_k b\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 (a^+=1\Rightarrow b^+=1)\ \wedge\ (a^-=1\Rightarrow b^-=1).
\]
Here $a^+$ and $a^-$ denote the positive and negative Boolean evidence bits.
Thus $\Nval\leq_k\Tval,\Fval\leq_k\Bval$, while $\Tval$ and $\Fval$ are
incomparable. A profile is \emph{persistent} if $wRu$ implies
$v(w)\leq_k v(u)$: neither evidence channel loses support.
This is an extra assumption on the chosen profile, not a theorem asserting
that every formula of 4PEL is persistent.

\begin{theorem}[S5 collapse under persistence]
On a reflexive, symmetric, transitive frame, a persistent profile is locally
constant everywhere. Moreover,
\[
 \sigma\in\mathcal N_{R,v}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \exists w\;\forall q\in\sigma\;(v(w)=q).
\]
In particular, no two distinct phase closures meet.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
If $wRu$, symmetry gives $uRw$. Persistence gives both
$v(w)\leq_k v(u)$ and $v(u)\leq_k v(w)$, hence equality by antisymmetry of
the information order. Reflexivity ensures each successor profile is exactly
the singleton $\{v(w)\}$. Apply the classification from Appendix~\ref{app:phase-nerve}.
\end{proof}

This is constancy within each accessibility component; disconnected components
may carry different values. Constant $\Bval$ profiles are permitted: stable
inconsistency does not violate the persistence assumption.

\begin{theorem}[Forced phase completion on S4 frames]
Let $R$ be reflexive and transitive and let $v$ be persistent. If $w$ lies in
both $\operatorname{Cl}(X_{\Tval})$ and $\operatorname{Cl}(X_{\Fval})$, then
$v(w)=\Nval$. Consequently, if a nerve face $\sigma$ contains both $\Tval$
and $\Fval$, then $\sigma\cup\{\Nval\}$ is also a face, witnessed at the same
world. In particular:
\begin{itemize}
\item a $\Tval$--$\Fval$ edge forces the filled $\Nval,\Tval,\Fval$ triangle;
\item a filled $\Tval,\Fval,\Bval$ triangle forces the full tetrahedron.
\end{itemize}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The two closure memberships supply successors of values $\Tval$ and $\Fval$.
Persistence makes $v(w)$ a common information lower bound of both. The only
such value is $\Nval$: positive evidence would exclude a $\Fval$ successor,
and negative evidence would exclude a $\Tval$ successor. Since every point
belongs to the closure of its own fibre, the same witness supplies the extra
$\Nval$ contact.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Sharpness: dimension three survives]
The full phase tetrahedron is realized by a persistent profile on a four-world
reflexive, transitive, antisymmetric frame.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Take the worlds $Q$, with actual profile $v(q)=q$. Let $\Nval$ see all four
worlds and let each of $\Tval,\Fval,\Bval$ see only itself. This relation is
reflexive, transitive and antisymmetric. Every edge out of $\Nval$ preserves
both evidence bits, and the other edges are loops. All four phases are
reachable at $\Nval$, giving the tetrahedron. The frame is not symmetric.
\end{proof}

Thus persistence imposes constraints on which faces can occur without larger
faces; it does not itself lower the maximal S4 nerve dimension. A complete
characterization of all persistent S4 nerves remains a separate target.
